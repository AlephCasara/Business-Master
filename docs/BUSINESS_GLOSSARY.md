# Business Glossary — Marketing, Ecommerce and Experiment Economics

This glossary exists so operators and agents use the same vocabulary. Terms are defined operationally: what the metric means, how it relates to neighboring metrics, and why Business Master cares.

---

## Core commercial terms

### Offer

The complete proposition presented to a customer, not just the underlying product.

An offer can include:
- promised outcome;
- product/service;
- price;
- payment terms;
- bonuses;
- guarantee / risk reversal;
- urgency/scarcity;
- proof;
- delivery mechanism;
- onboarding/next step.

Two sellers can sell the same product with different offers and get very different conversion rates.

### Funnel

The ordered set of customer steps from first exposure to purchase and later monetization.

Example:

```text
ad/video
→ landing page
→ checkout
→ order bump
→ upsell
→ fulfillment
→ retention / next offer
```

### Front-end

The first offer/purchase used to acquire a customer.

A front-end may intentionally have a low margin if backend monetization produces positive LTV economics.

### Backend

Revenue generated after the initial purchase, such as upsells, subscriptions, later offers or repeat purchases.

### Low ticket / High ticket

Informal relative price categories.

- `low ticket`: inexpensive purchase, usually lower purchase friction and lower margin per buyer;
- `high ticket`: expensive purchase, often requiring more trust, qualification or sales interaction.

There is no universal numeric boundary; use market context.

### Order bump

Small complementary offer presented at checkout, usually accepted with one additional action.

### Upsell

Offer presented after or alongside a purchase that increases total customer value, often a larger/better/complementary product.

### Downsell

Lower-price or reduced-scope offer presented after a customer rejects a more expensive offer.

## Acquisition metrics

### Impression

One eligible display of an ad, thumbnail or content unit. Platform definitions differ.

### Reach

Number of distinct users/accounts exposed. One user can create several impressions.

### CPM — Cost per Mille

Cost per 1,000 impressions.

```text
CPM = ad spend / impressions × 1000
```

CPM mostly describes the price of accessing an audience, not whether the ad converts.

### CTR — Click-through rate

Fraction of impressions that produced a click.

```text
CTR = clicks / impressions
```

A high CTR can mean strong creative/targeting, but can still produce bad business if the clicks are low intent.

### CPC — Cost per click

```text
CPC = ad spend / clicks
```

CPC is influenced by CPM and CTR.

A useful identity:

```text
CPC ≈ CPM / (1000 × CTR)
```

when CTR is expressed as a fraction.

### CVR / Conversion rate

Fraction of a defined population that completes the target conversion.

Examples:
- landing-page purchase CVR = purchases / landing sessions;
- checkout CVR = purchases / checkout starts;
- lead CVR = leads / visitors.

Always record denominator/context.

### CPA — Cost per acquisition/action

Cost to produce a specified action.

```text
CPA = spend / conversions
```

`conversion` must be explicit: lead, sale, booked call, app install, etc.

### CAC — Customer acquisition cost

Total acquisition cost divided by new customers.

At minimum:

```text
CAC = acquisition spend / new customers
```

A more complete CAC may include sales labor, tooling and creative costs.

### ROAS — Return on ad spend

```text
ROAS = attributed revenue / ad spend
```

ROAS is not profit. A 2.0 ROAS can be excellent or disastrous depending on gross margin, refunds, fulfillment, tax and backend revenue.

### MER — Marketing efficiency ratio

Blended company/store revenue divided by total marketing spend.

```text
MER = total revenue / total marketing spend
```

Useful when attribution across channels becomes noisy.

## Customer economics

### AOV — Average order value

Average revenue per order.

```text
AOV = revenue / number of orders
```

Order bumps and upsells can increase AOV.

### ARPU — Average revenue per user

```text
ARPU = revenue / users
```

The exact time window must be specified.

### LTV / CLV — Lifetime value

Expected economic value generated by a customer over their relationship with the business.

There are several definitions. Business Master should distinguish at least:
- lifetime revenue;
- lifetime gross profit/contribution.

For acquisition decisions, profit-based LTV is usually more useful than revenue LTV.

### LTV:CAC

Ratio of customer lifetime value to acquisition cost.

```text
LTV:CAC = LTV / CAC
```

It describes how much value is generated relative to acquisition cost, but long payback periods can still create cashflow problems.

### Payback period

Time required for customer contribution to recover CAC.

Two businesses with the same LTV:CAC can have very different working-capital requirements if one pays back tomorrow and the other in a year.

### Repeat purchase rate

Fraction of customers who purchase again in a defined period.

### Churn

Fraction of customers/subscribers who leave during a period.

For recurring revenue, churn strongly affects LTV.

## Margin and ecommerce economics

### Revenue

Money recognized from sales before subtracting costs.

### GMV — Gross merchandise value

Total monetary value of merchandise transacted through a marketplace/store during a period.

GMV is not seller revenue or profit when commissions, refunds and supplier economics differ.

### COGS — Cost of goods sold

Direct cost of the product sold.

For physical commerce this may include supplier unit cost and other directly attributable product costs.

### Gross profit

```text
gross profit = revenue - COGS
```

Definitions can vary; define included costs explicitly.

### Gross margin

```text
gross margin = gross profit / revenue
```

### Contribution margin

Revenue remaining after **variable costs attributable to the transaction**.

A practical ecommerce definition may be:

```text
selling price
- supplier cost
- marketplace/payment fees
- shipping/logistics paid by seller
- variable fulfillment
- variable ad/acquisition cost
- expected returns/refund reserve
= contribution
```

Taxes may be modeled separately depending on jurisdiction/accounting mode.

Business Master prefers contribution over gross spread for go/no-go decisions.

### Gross spread

Simple difference between sale price and purchase/supplier price.

```text
spread = sale price - supplier price
```

This is **not profit** because it ignores platform fees, shipping, returns, tax, ads and other costs.

### Working capital

Cash tied up in operating the business before settlement returns it.

For resale:

```text
supplier payment at D0
→ item/order in transit
→ customer delivery
→ marketplace payout
```

The longer the cycle, the more capital is required to maintain the same sales rate.

### Cash lock

Time between cash leaving the business and cash becoming available again.

### Capital velocity

Informal measure of return relative to capital and time locked.

A useful experiment metric can be:

```text
capital_velocity ≈ contribution / (working capital × lock days)
```

Do not use it when contribution is negative.

## Affiliate metrics

### Commission

Amount or percentage paid to an affiliate for an attributed conversion.

### EPC — Earnings per click

```text
EPC = affiliate earnings / outbound clicks
```

EPC makes offers easier to compare when traffic sources are similar.

### Cookie / attribution window

Period and rules under which a conversion is credited to an affiliate.

These are platform-specific and can materially change economics.

## Content metrics

### Views

Platform-defined qualified plays/views. Definitions differ across YouTube, TikTok and Instagram.

### Watch time

Total time viewers spent watching.

### Average view duration (AVD)

Average amount of time watched per play.

### Average percentage viewed (APV)

Average fraction of video watched.

```text
APV ≈ average view duration / video duration
```

Platform-specific handling of loops and replays can differ.

### Retention curve

Fraction of viewers still watching at each point in the video.

Useful for identifying weak openings, pacing problems and specific drop-off points.

### Hook rate

Non-standard operational metric measuring whether the opening prevents immediate swipes/drop-offs. Define the exact platform-specific denominator before using it.

### Shares per view

```text
shares / views
```

Often more informative than raw likes for content with genuine forwarding value.

### Followers/subscribers per view

```text
new followers / views
```

Measures how effectively a piece turns reach into owned audience.

### RPM — Revenue per mille

Revenue per 1,000 views/impressions under a platform monetization program.

RPM is an outcome, not guaranteed by niche claims.

## Paid media operational terms

### Creative

The actual ad/content asset shown to a user: video, image, copy, hook, CTA and associated presentation.

### Creative angle

The underlying argument/frame used by the creative.

Examples:
- pain avoidance;
- transformation;
- social proof;
- comparison;
- speed/convenience;
- novelty;
- identity/status.

Several creatives can express one angle.

### Creative fatigue / saturation

Performance degradation as an audience sees the same creative/angle repeatedly or competitors copy it.

Business Master should model angle lineage and saturation rather than treating each generated file as independent.

### Campaign / ad set / ad

Meta Ads hierarchy commonly uses:

```text
Campaign
  → Ad Set
      → Ad
```

Campaign stores objective/configuration, ad set often contains audience/budget/bidding configuration, and ad contains creative.

### CBO — Campaign Budget Optimization

Meta-style budget allocation where campaign-level budget is distributed across ad sets by the platform.

Treat as a media-buying parameter, not an invariant.

### Bid Cap

Bidding control placing a ceiling/constraint on auction bids.

Its usefulness depends on auction dynamics, account data, offer economics and platform changes. It should be tested, never encoded as a universal winning recipe.

## Experimentation terms

### Hypothesis

A falsifiable economic claim.

Example:

> Short chart-based videos about X can generate above-baseline retention and clicks to a paid chart pack.

### Experiment

A bounded exposure designed to collect evidence about a hypothesis.

### PROBE

Cheapest practical real-world test. Goal: verify plumbing and/or obtain first external evidence with minimal blast radius.

### PILOT

Replicated or stronger test after a promising probe. Goal: test repeatability and economics under more realistic volume.

### SCALE

Material resource allocation after repeated evidence. Scale is not a reward for one outlier.

### Information gain

How much an experiment is expected to reduce uncertainty relevant to future decisions.

### Feedback speed

How quickly useful evidence returns.

### Parallelizability

Ability to run more independent experiments without requiring proportional human attention.

### Baseline

Expected performance for a comparable cohort: same platform, format, age, channel maturity and other material context.

### Mutation

Child experiment that deliberately changes one or more dimensions from a parent while preserving lineage.

### Winner replication

Re-running the structure of a promising result with enough controlled variation to determine whether performance repeats.

## Relationship map

A simplified paid-commerce chain:

```text
CPM
  + creative quality
      ↓
     CTR
      ↓
     CPC
      + landing/offer quality
          ↓
         CVR
          ↓
         CPA / CAC
          + AOV / backend / repeats
              ↓
             LTV
              + COGS / fees / fulfillment / returns
                  ↓
             contribution / profit
```

A simplified organic-content chain:

```text
impressions
→ views
→ retention
→ engagement/shares
→ followers/clicks
→ lead/order
→ revenue
→ contribution/LTV
```

Business Master should preserve every layer rather than jumping from `views` directly to `profitability`.
